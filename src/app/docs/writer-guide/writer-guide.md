# Writer's Chapter Playbook: Math & Science Step-by-Step Examples

This playbook provides concrete, end-to-end examples for content teams to execute the 5-step curriculum writing guide. It contains one complete chapter blueprint for **Mathematics (Solving One-Step Equations)** and one for **Science (Photosynthesis)**.

---

## 📐 Playbook Example 1: Mathematics
### Topic: Solving One-Step Linear Equations (e.g., $x + 5 = 12$)

### Step 1: Map the Micro-Skill Dependency Chain
Writers must define what foundational skills are required before introducing the new concept.

```text
[Prerequisite: Arithmetic Arithmetic Facts] ➔ [Prerequisite: Variables as Placeholders]
                                          |
                                          v
                         [Micro-Skill 1: The Balance Scale Concept]
                                          |
                                          v
                         [Micro-Skill 2: Inverse Operations]
                                          |
                                          v
                         [Micro-Skill 3: Isolating the Variable]
```

1. **Prerequisite Nodes:** 
   * Student knows addition/subtraction facts up to 20.
   * Student understands that a letter like $x$ represents an unknown number (placeholder).
2. **Target Micro-Skills for this Node:**
   * **Micro-Skill 1:** The **Equality Balance** (Both sides of an equation must stay equal).
   * **Micro-Skill 2:** **Inverse Operations** (Subtraction undoes addition; addition undoes subtraction).
   * **Micro-Skill 3:** **Isolating the Variable** (Getting the unknown letter completely by itself).

---

### Step 2: Define the "Anchor Analogy"
Writers must create a visual, non-abstract analogy that explains the concept to a beginner.

* **The Pan Balance Scale Analogy:**
  "Imagine a physical balance scale that is perfectly level. On the left side of the scale, we have a mystery present box ($x$) and 5 marble weights. On the right side, we have 12 marble weights. Because the scale is balanced, we know the mystery box plus the 5 marbles weighs exactly the same as the 12 marbles on the other side."

```text
       [Mystery Box]                   
         [ x ]  ●●               ●●●●●●
         ●●●                     ●●●●●●
   ======================     ======================
   \________L1_________/      \________R1_________/
            \                     /
             \_____/\    /\______/
                     \  /
                      \/ [Balanced Scale]
```

* **The Rule of Action:**
  "If we want to know the weight of the mystery box, we need to get it by itself. If we just swipe away the 5 marbles on the left side, the scale will tip! To keep the scale perfectly balanced, we must perform the **exact same action** on the other side—swipe away 5 marbles from the right side too!"

---

### Step 3: Define the "Misconception Registry"
Writers must document the common math traps and map them to incorrect answer choices.

* **Equation to Solve:** $x + 5 = 12$
* **Registry Table:**

| Code | Misconception / Trap | Student Logic | Distractor Choice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **M-MATH-01** | **Adding instead of Subtracting** | Student sees the $+$ sign and automatically adds the numbers: $12 + 5$. | **17** |
| **M-MATH-02** | **Operand Confusion** | Student multiplies instead of subtracts, thinking they need to combine them: $12 \times 5$. | **60** |
| **M-MATH-03** | **Sign/Direction Error** | Student subtracts the larger number from the smaller one: $5 - 12$. | **-7** |

---

### Step 4: Author the Scaffolding Questions
Writers must create questions at three distinct cognitive levels (Easy, Medium, Hard).

#### 🟢 Easy Question (Concrete/Visual Helper)
* **Question:** "Look at the balanced scale below. On the left, there is a box $x$ and 3 weights. On the right, there are 8 weights. If we remove 3 weights from both sides to find $x$, how many weights are left on the right side?"
* **Visual Assist:** A simple diagram showing $x + 3$ balanced with 8.
* **Choices:** A) 3 weights, B) 5 weights (Correct), C) 8 weights, D) 11 weights.

#### 🟡 Medium Question (Standard Calculation)
* **Question:** "Solve for $y$: $y + 7 = 19$"
* **Choices:** A) 26 (Trap M-MATH-01), B) 12 (Correct), C) 133 (Trap M-MATH-02), D) -12 (Trap M-MATH-03).

#### 🔴 Hard Question (Multi-Step Word Puzzle)
* **Question:** "A mystery bag of candy and 4 loose pieces weigh the same as 3 bags of 5 candy pieces each. How many candy pieces are in the mystery bag?"
* **Mathematical Translation:** $x + 4 = 3 \times 5 \rightarrow x + 4 = 15$.
* **Choices:** A) 11 pieces (Correct), B) 19 pieces, C) 15 pieces, D) 9 pieces.

---

### Step 5: Write the Adaptive Remediation Scripts
Writers must script the dialogue a virtual tutor would say to help a student who triggered a misconception.

* **Remediation Trigger: Student answered "17" for $x + 5 = 12$ (Trap M-MATH-01)**
* **Tutor Script:**
  "Tutor: Oh, I see what happened! You saw the plus sign in $x + 5$ and decided to add $12 + 5$ to get 17. 
  Let's test that out in our balance scale. If the mystery box weighs 17, then the left side is $17 + 5$, which weighs 22. But the right side only weighs 12! The scale would tip!
  
  To undo addition, we have to use its opposite operation: **subtraction**. Let's subtract 5 from both sides of the scale to keep it balanced:
  $$x + 5 - 5 = 12 - 5$$
  $$x = 7$$
  Does $7 + 5 = 12$ make the scale balance? Yes! You got it."

## 🧪 Playbook Example 2: Science
### Topic: Photosynthesis (Inputs, Outputs, and Energy Transformation)

### Step 1: Map the Micro-Skill Dependency Chain
Writers define the cognitive path for understanding how plants generate food.

```text
[Prerequisite: Plant Structures] ➔ [Prerequisite: Basic Needs of Life]
                                      |
                                      v
                     [Micro-Skill 1: Inputs of Photosynthesis]
                                      |
                                      v
                     [Micro-Skill 2: Outputs of Photosynthesis]
                                      |
                                      v
                     [Micro-Skill 3: Energy Conversion in Chloroplasts]
```

1. **Prerequisite Nodes:**
   * Student knows that plants have roots, stems, and leaves.
   * Student knows that animals eat food for energy, but plants do not eat animals.
2. **Target Micro-Skills for this Node:**
   * **Micro-Skill 1:** Identify water, carbon dioxide, and sunlight as the **inputs** (ingredients).
   * **Micro-Skill 2:** Identify glucose (sugar) and oxygen as the **outputs** (products).
   * **Micro-Skill 3:** Understand that **chloroplasts** act as solar panels transforming light energy into chemical food energy.

---

### Step 2: Define the "Anchor Analogy"
Writers create a narrative analogy to explain the microscopic biological process.

* **The Solar-Powered Leaf Kitchen Analogy:**
  "Think of a green leaf as a tiny, busy **kitchen** inside a restaurant. 
  * The chefs in the kitchen are the **chloroplasts**.
  * The kitchen needs two raw ingredients (Inputs): **water** (sucked up from the ground by roots) and **carbon dioxide** gas (absorbed from the air through tiny leaf pores called *stomata*).
  * To bake their food, the chefs need heat. They turn on their solar ovens powered by **sunlight** (energy input).
  * Once the cooking is finished, the chefs produce two things (Outputs): a delicious **glucose cake** (sugar used as food for the plant) and **oxygen gas** (waste steam from the cooking). Because the leaf chefs don't need oxygen, they release it out into the air for us to breathe!"

```text
                 [ Sun (Power Source) ]
                          |
                          v
   CO2 (Air) ➔ 🟩🟩 [ Leaf Kitchen ] 🟩🟩 ➔ Oxygen (Waste)
                          ^
                          |
                     Water (Roots)
```

---

### Step 3: Define the "Misconception Registry"
Writers document core plant biology misunderstandings and translate them to distractors.

* **Core Question Concept:** Where do plants get their food/mass?
* **Registry Table:**

| Code | Misconception / Trap | Student Logic | Distractor Choice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **M-SCI-01** | **Plants "eat" soil** | Student believes plant roots swallow soil nutrients to build the plant body. | **Soil / Dirt** |
| **M-SCI-02** | **Photosynthesis is plant respiration** | Student thinks plants breathe carbon dioxide instead of oxygen to survive, not realizing respiration is separate. | **Plants do not need Oxygen** |
| **M-SCI-03** | **Light is matter** | Student treats light as a chemical molecule rather than an energy trigger. | **Light is a physical output** |

---

### Step 4: Author the Scaffolding Questions
Writers create progressively deeper evaluations of leaf chemistry.

#### 🟢 Easy Question (Concrete/Visual Helper)
* **Question:** "A plant leaf has tiny mouth-pores called stomata. According to the Leaf Kitchen analogy, what gas enters these pores from the air as a main ingredient for cooking?"
* **Visual Assist:** A labeled leaf drawing showing gases entering and leaving.
* **Choices:** A) Oxygen, B) Carbon dioxide (Correct), C) Water, D) Nitrogen.

#### 🟡 Medium Question (Standard Knowledge)
* **Question:** "What are the chemical outputs (products) of photosynthesis?"
* **Choices:** A) Carbon dioxide and water, B) Glucose sugar and oxygen (Correct), C) Soil nutrients and sunlight (Trap M-SCI-01), D) Oxygen and water.

#### 🔴 Hard Question (Higher-Order Thinking / Application)
* **Question:** "A healthy potted plant is placed under a sealed glass jar with soil and water. The jar is placed in a sunny room, but covered with a thick cardboard box so it is in total darkness. What will happen to the carbon dioxide levels inside the jar after 24 hours?"
* **Scientific Reasoning:** Without light, photosynthesis stops (no CO2 consumption). However, the plant still performs respiration (CO2 release). Thus, CO2 levels will rise.
* **Choices:** A) Carbon dioxide levels will decrease, B) Carbon dioxide levels will increase (Correct), C) Carbon dioxide levels will stay exactly the same, D) Carbon dioxide will turn into soil (Trap M-SCI-01).

---

### Step 5: Write the Adaptive Remediation Scripts
Writers script the feedback for students who get stuck on the misconceptions.

* **Remediation Trigger: Student answered "Soil / Dirt" to where plants get food (Trap M-SCI-01)**
* **Tutor Script:**
  "Tutor: Ah! It is extremely easy to think that plants eat soil because we bury their roots in dirt. But let's think about a famous scientific experiment:
  
  A scientist planted a willow tree in a pot containing 200 pounds of dry soil. After 5 years of watering, the tree grew to weigh 169 pounds! But when the scientist dried and weighed the soil again, the soil had only lost 2 ounces of weight!
  
  If the tree grew by 169 pounds, but the soil only lost 2 ounces, the plant couldn't have built itself out of the soil! It built its entire body out of **carbon dioxide gas** from the air and **water**! Soil nutrients are just like vitamins—they help, but they are not the main food."
